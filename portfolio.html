Built Solo  ·  Case Study 01
LIVEapplystudio.net
Apply Studio
AI job application platform. From a job post to a tailored resume, cover letter, and answers in under 90 seconds.
<90s per application20+ jobs in parallel100+ job boards
The problem
A serious job application takes 2 to 4 hours to do well: a keyword-matched resume, a tailored cover letter, and custom answers for every role. So most candidates either apply generically and get filtered out, or spend days on a handful of roles. Multi-agent orchestration makes it possible to parallelize that rewriting across dozens of postings at once, compressing days of work into minutes.
Who it’s for
Early career
The broad applier
Applying widely and needs to stand out without a long track record.
Career switcher
The re-framer
Needs existing experience re-positioned for a new domain or function.
Senior IC
The selective closer
Applies to fewer roles but wants each one tailored, ATS-ready, and fast.
How I solve it
  • An in-house web scraper that outperforms third-party tools on hard job sites, extracting descriptions, requirements, and application questions.
  • A multi-agent workflow across GPT, Claude, and Gemini, with one agent per job running in parallel.
  • A bullet-by-bullet accept, edit, reject UI so the user keeps their voice while the manual grind disappears.
  • Real-time ATS scoring and keyword-gap analysis across 20+ jobs before submission. Built and shipped solo in under 6 weeks.
Results
Per-application time cut from ~3 hours to under 90 seconds.
20+ tailored applications generated in under one hour.
ATS keyword coverage surfaced before every submission.

Built Solo  ·  Case Study 02
LIVEthecardscout.app
Card Scout
AI credit-card optimizer. Real math on your real spending, not affiliate rankings.
$2,500+ avg annual value100+ cards analyzed2 min vs hours
The problem
The average household leaves $1,500 to $3,000 in credit-card rewards unclaimed each year. Most comparison sites are revenue-optimized for banks, not users, surfacing the highest affiliate payout rather than the highest actual value for a given spending profile. Combining a structured valuation engine with multi-model reasoning personalizes recommendations at a level no static site can match.
Who it’s for  (three entry points, mapped to expertise)
Beginner
“I’m starting out”
No cards yet. Answers 3 questions and gets ranked matches for goals and spend.
Intermediate
“I have cards already”
Wants to see the gaps in a current wallet and exactly what to add next.
Advanced
“I know what I want”
Browses and filters 100+ cards by issuer, rewards type, and annual fee.
How I solve it
  • Treat rewards as a valuation problem, not a search problem, modeling expected annual value across 100+ cards per user profile.
  • A TypeScript valuation engine computes expected value per card and per card combination from spend and category multipliers.
  • Bank-specific crawlers keep card data, APRs, and bonus structures fresh automatically, so nothing goes stale.
  • Multi-model orchestration (GPT, Claude, DeepSeek) for reasoning and explanations, with transparent ranked results and full earnings breakdowns.
Results
Average session surfaces $2,500+ in net annual card value.
Research time cut from hours to under 2 minutes.
100+ cards analyzed per session, with the “why” shown for every rank.

Built Solo  ·  Case Study 03
LIVEneuralmob.xyz
Neural Mob
Ask once, three minds answer. A multi-model debate engine with a judge and visible reasoning.
3 models debate1 scoring judge100% reasoning visible
The problem
A single model optimizes for plausibility, not correctness. There is no forcing function and no adversarial pressure, so you get confident answers that can be subtly wrong. Multi-agent debate is a known research technique but is rarely productized with user-visible transparency. The tooling now exists to route a query to several models in parallel, collect structured outputs, and score reasoning quality at production speed.
Who it’s for
Builders & PMs
The model comparer
Wants to see how GPT, Claude, and Gemini differ before trusting one for a task.
Decision-makers
The high-stakes asker
Needs a higher-confidence answer on an ambiguous, consequential question.
AI power users
The skeptic
Does not trust one model and wants a judged, fully transparent consensus.
How I solve it
  • Agent roles, system prompts, and model assignments defined for parallel debate routing, so each agent brings distinct priors.
  • A scoring judge evaluates every response against a rubric that penalizes hedging and rewards specificity and evidence.
  • Full attribution and reasoning traces on the result surface, so users see exactly why the winning answer won.
  • Graceful degradation when an agent times out or returns malformed output. This is core AI PM craft: agent design, failure modes, scoring non-deterministic outputs, and building trust.
Results
Adversarial debate surfaces stronger answers than any single model.
Every answer ships with a traceable scoring rationale.
Built and deployed solo. Live product with real users.

Built Solo  ·  Case Study 04
LIVEstock-tracker-chi-one.vercel.app
StockTracker
AI market intelligence. Real-time data from 20 sources, synthesized by Claude and Kimi K2 into a 90-second daily brief.
20 live data sources6 AI features49 API routes
The problem
Retail investors drown in market data but starve for synthesis. Prices, filings, analyst ratings, and 24/7 news arrive faster than anyone can read them, and the tools that aggregate them either dump raw feeds or hide the reasoning behind a paywalled black box. The result is information without judgment, with no way to tell what the market has already priced in. Cheap, fast inference plus prompt caching finally make it economical to run AI synthesis over hundreds of articles a day per user.
Who it’s for
Active trader
The signal seeker
Wants to know why a stock moved and whether the news is already priced in.
Long-term investor
The portfolio watcher
Tracks a watchlist and wants a daily brief, not a 24/7 firehose.
Quant-curious
The screener
Filters the whole market in plain English and backtests ideas fast.
How I solve it
  • A two-model AI pipeline: Claude Haiku tags 200+ articles a day, Kimi K2 synthesizes them into a 90-second brief.
  • Source-tier weighting and a “priced-in” heuristic (news age × price move × sentiment) so users trust what they read.
  • A natural-language screener that compiles plain English into database filters at ~90% accuracy, zero fine-tuning.
  • Cost as a product surface: every AI call metered to a Postgres ledger, gated by feature flags, billed against credits. 49 API routes and a 22-table schema, shipped solo.
Results
200+ articles a day compressed into a readable 90-second AI brief.
49 API routes and a 22-table Postgres schema, deployed solo, 65/65 tests green.
Every AI call metered, budgeted, and billed with a full audit trail.
